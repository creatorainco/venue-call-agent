# Loopback soak, 2026-11-20: the TTS ceiling and barge-in fixes (it-22)

Branch `fix/venue-call-tts-ceiling-and-bargein`, off `origin/dev` at 8d98f07. Loopback soak only (`npm run soak:corpus`, fake legs, mock seam, real Google STT, Gemini and TTS). No carrier, no Twilio, no call placed. The JSON of every run sits beside this file: `2026-11-20-base-r1..3.json` are `origin/dev`, and `2026-11-20-fix-r1..2.json` are this branch.

## What was broken

Three faults, found by the it-22 spike (`spike/tts-stall` at db1ea8a).

1. **The voicemail script never rendered.** Google TTS (Chirp 3 HD) takes 4.9 to 5.1 s for the mock's 498-character voicemail script. The ceiling was a flat 4000 ms with no retry, so the request was aborted every time. Every baseline call logged `prerender_failed TtsUnavailable`, and a failure is not cached. A call that then needed the script ended with `internal_error TtsUnavailable` (phone-menu in all three baseline runs, voicemail in one).
2. **Barge-in never cut the opening.** The corpus bed has an RMS of about 820. The energy detector's pinned end threshold is 600. After the venue's first word the detector stayed in speech for the rest of the call, so it never ended a turn and never started another. A reply then queued behind the agent's own sentences: up to 17.5 s in the instrumented spike run (`behind=sentence`).
3. **An early "Hello?" split the turn.** The bridge ends a turn early on the interim "Hello?". When the final then came back as a new turn ("Hello? Hello, who is this?"), its answer waited behind the whole opening the agent had started for the early half.

## The fix

- `synthesisCeilingMs` (`src/speech/tts.ts`): max(4000 ms, 1500 ms + 12 ms a character), from `TTS.timeoutBaseMs` and `TTS.timeoutPerCharMs`, capped at 10000 ms (`TTS.timeoutMaxMs`). The deadline covers the response body as well as the headers. The voicemail script gets 7476 ms. Every sentence of 208 characters or fewer keeps 4000 ms.
- `RoomFloor` (`src/carrier/vad.ts`): the room's floor, a low percentile of the last 3 s of frame energy. `ForegroundSpeech` used this logic inline and now uses the helper. `TurnDetector` takes it as an option. A frame starts or holds a turn only when it is also 9 dB over the floor. `CascadeBridge` passes `TURN_END` by default. `ScriptedBridge` and `OutputGate` keep the fixed thresholds.
- `CascadeBridge.onFinal`: a final that is a new turn after an early end, while the agent is speaking, bumps the generation counter that barge-in already uses. The early reply's sentences not yet on the leg are dropped. The sentence playing finishes, and the disclosure is never dropped. The bridge logs `superseded_early_reply`.
- One log line kept from the spike: `speak_wait ms=<n> behind=<none|sentence|disclosure> sentences=<n>`. It says how long a reply waited and what it waited behind. It carries numbers and a tag, never text.

## Reading

- The fixed failures are gone in both runs: no `prerender_failed`, and no call ended on `internal_error`. The worst synthesis wait fell from 9.1 to 17.5 s to 2.3 to 3.1 s. Each fix run's worst wait is a reply behind one sentence already playing (`speak_wait behind=sentence`).
- First audio p50 and p90 and dead air p90 sit inside the baseline's own spread, or beside it. fix-r1's p90 of 4318 ms is above every baseline. Its slow turns (money-minimum-spend, who-pays, they-interrupt, money-covered-amount) are recogniser endpointing (`endpointing_wire` up to 4.4 s), which this change does not touch. fix-r2's p90 is 2968 ms. Five runs cannot separate this change from that noise, so it ships as a bug fix, not as a latency change.
- The cold-start gate still fails, at 1590 and 1652 ms against 1000 ms. That is a separate open item.
- The spike's stall-r2 showed a third shape this change does not fix: a second final with no early end before it ("Golden Crane, Marcus speaking.") queued 11.7 s behind the opening. Dropping the queued tail of any reply on a new venue turn would cover it. Whether an opening beat may go unsaid is a product call, so it is left out.
- Real calls carry no synthetic bed. Whether the 9 dB floor holds against a real kitchen, the echo of our own voice and a carrier's comfort noise needs a real call, and was not measured here.
